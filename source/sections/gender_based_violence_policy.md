# Gender-Based Violence and the Workplace Policy

VisibleHand is committed to a workplace where workforce members affected by gender-based violence can get support, keep their jobs, and stay safe. This policy describes how VisibleHand shares information about gender-based violence, refers employee-survivors to services, protects them from discrimination and retaliation, and complies with the leave, accommodation, and confidentiality requirements of the law.

This policy is adopted in part to satisfy New York State Finance Law §139-M, which requires bidders on New York State competitive procurements to have and implement a written policy addressing gender-based violence and the workplace. It works together with the Sexual Harassment Prevention Policy, which governs harassment occurring in the course of VisibleHand work.

## Applicable Standards and Legal Requirements

* New York State Finance Law §139-M - Gender-Based Violence and the Workplace Certification
* New York State Human Rights Law (Executive Law, Article 15), including its protections for domestic violence victim status and its requirement of reasonable accommodations for victims
* New York Labor Law §196-b - Paid Sick and Safe Leave
* New York City Earned Safe and Sick Time Act, where it applies
* Colorado Healthy Families and Workplaces Act, C.R.S. §8-13.3-401 et seq.
* C.R.S. §24-34-402.7 - Leave for victims of domestic abuse, stalking, sexual assault, and related crimes
* All other applicable state and local laws in jurisdictions where VisibleHand workforce members live or work

VisibleHand follows the laws of every state and city where its workforce members work. Where those laws differ, the law that gives the workforce member more protection applies.

## Scope

This policy applies to all VisibleHand employees, applicants, supervisors, and officers, whether they work in an office, remotely, at a customer facility, or while traveling on company business. It also covers conduct by contractors and visitors that affects VisibleHand workforce members at work.

## Definitions

* **Gender-based violence** is any form of violence or abuse directed at a person because of their sex, gender, gender identity, or gender expression, or that disproportionately affects people of a particular gender. It includes domestic violence, intimate partner violence, dating violence, sexual assault, stalking, sexual harassment, human trafficking, and technology-facilitated abuse such as harassing messages or location tracking.
* **Employee-survivor** is a workforce member who is, or has been, a victim or survivor of gender-based violence, whether the violence happened at work or elsewhere, and whether it is current or past.
* **Disclosure** is a workforce member telling a supervisor, an officer, or the Policy Administrator that they are an employee-survivor.
* **Policy Administrator** is the person responsible for this policy. Until VisibleHand designates a human resources lead, the Policy Administrator is the Chief Executive Officer, currently Matt Morris (matt@visiblehand.io, 720-500-5369).

## Sharing Information

VisibleHand keeps information about gender-based violence where every workforce member can see and access it. At a minimum, VisibleHand:

1. Displays the New York State Domestic and Sexual Violence Hotline information (call 800-942-6906, text 844-997-2121, or chat at opdv.ny.gov; 711 for Deaf or hard of hearing) in every VisibleHand office or shared workspace.
2. Displays the New York State Office for the Prevention of Domestic Violence (OPDV) "Gender-Based Violence and the Workplace" poster alongside other required employment postings.
3. Posts this policy, the hotline information, and the poster in the company's shared drive and in this policy repository, so that remote workforce members have the same access as those working in an office.
4. Gives each workforce member a copy of this policy at hire and whenever it is updated, and includes it in the VisibleHand Employee Handbook.
5. Publishes the local and national resources listed below alongside the New York hotline information.

## Referral of Employee-Survivors to Services

When a workforce member discloses current or past victim status, the supervisor or officer who receives the disclosure must refer that person to services. This referral is required every time; it is not discretionary.

1. Workforce members who live or work in New York State are referred to the New York State Domestic and Sexual Violence Hotline and, where helpful, to a local New York service provider.
2. Workforce members who live or work outside New York State are referred to a local service provider or to a statewide or national hotline, including those listed below.
3. The referral is given verbally and in writing, for example by handing over or emailing the resource list, and the Policy Administrator is notified that a referral was made.
4. VisibleHand must offer the referral, but the workforce member is not required to contact or use any service. Declining services never affects a person's job, assignments, or standing.
5. Supervisors receiving a disclosure listen, do not ask for proof or for details of the violence, ask what support the person needs, and remind them of their rights under this policy.

## Prohibition on Discrimination and Retaliation

VisibleHand prohibits discrimination or retaliation against any workforce member or applicant who is, or is perceived to be, a victim or survivor of gender-based violence.

VisibleHand will not refuse to hire, terminate, demote, discipline, reduce pay or hours, reassign, or otherwise treat a workforce member worse because they:

1. Are or were a victim or survivor of gender-based violence;
2. Disclosed that status, or chose not to disclose it;
3. Requested or used leave, a schedule change, or another accommodation related to gender-based violence;
4. Sought or declined services, obtained a protective order, or took part in a court case; or
5. Reported a concern under this policy, or helped someone else do so.

A workforce member who believes they have been treated unfairly under this policy reports it to the Policy Administrator or, where the concern involves that person, to the Security Officer, currently Ian Schreuder, CTO (ian@visiblehand.io). VisibleHand investigates promptly, using the investigation procedure described in the Sexual Harassment Prevention Policy. Anyone who retaliates is subject to corrective disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.

## Leave, Accommodations, Confidentiality, and Safety

1. **New York.** For workforce members who live or work in New York State, this policy follows the New York State Human Rights Law, including its protections for domestic violence victim status and its requirement of reasonable accommodations for victims; New York State paid sick and safe leave under Labor Law §196-b; the New York City Earned Safe and Sick Time Act where it applies; and all other applicable New York laws and regulations.
2. **Colorado.** For workforce members who work in Colorado, this policy follows the Colorado Healthy Families and Workplaces Act, which allows paid sick leave for needs related to domestic abuse, sexual assault, or stalking, and C.R.S. §24-34-402.7, which provides protected leave for victims of domestic abuse, stalking, sexual assault, and related crimes where it applies.
3. **Other states.** Workforce members elsewhere receive the leave and protections their state and local laws provide.
4. **Leave and accommodations.** Employee-survivors may use available leave to seek medical care, counseling, or victim services, to relocate, to obtain a protective order, or to attend court. VisibleHand also considers reasonable changes such as a revised work schedule, remote work, a changed phone number or email address, screening of calls, and assistance documenting workplace incidents. VisibleHand requests reasonable documentation only as the law allows, and a statement from the workforce member or from an advocate is generally sufficient.
5. **Confidentiality.** Information about a workforce member's disclosure, leave, or accommodation is kept confidential and is shared only with those who need it to provide support or to keep the workplace safe, or as required by law. VisibleHand tells the workforce member before sharing information whenever that is possible.
6. **Safety at work.** Where a workforce member has a protective order that covers the workplace, or where a threat reaches the workplace, VisibleHand works with that person on a safety plan and contacts law enforcement when needed. Where the workplace is a customer facility, VisibleHand coordinates with the customer only to the extent necessary for safety and with the workforce member's knowledge.

## Implementation Support for Supervisors

The New York State Office for the Prevention of Domestic Violence offers free technical assistance to employers developing and implementing gender-based violence workplace policies. OPDV can be reached at workplace@opdv.ny.gov.

VisibleHand provides this OPDV contact information to every supervisor, to anyone performing human resources functions, and to the Policy Administrator. It is included in supervisor onboarding and in the annual review of this policy. Supervisors also receive a short briefing on how to respond to a disclosure and how to make the referral required by this policy.

3. Every workforce member acknowledges receipt of this policy at hire and after each update.
