# Sexual Harassment Prevention Policy

VisibleHand is committed to maintaining a workplace free from sexual harassment. Sexual harassment is a form of workplace discrimination and employee misconduct, and VisibleHand will not tolerate it from or toward any workforce member, customer, vendor, or other person doing business with VisibleHand. This policy describes the conduct that is prohibited, how to report it, how VisibleHand investigates reports, the sanctions that apply, and the external remedies available to every workforce member.

This policy is adopted in part to satisfy New York Labor Law §201-g, and it is the written policy VisibleHand relies upon when certifying compliance with New York State Finance Law §139-l as a bidder on New York State competitive procurements.

## Applicable Standards and Legal Requirements

* New York Labor Law §201-g - Sexual Harassment Prevention Policy and Training
* New York State Finance Law §139-l - Sexual Harassment Prevention Certification
* New York State Human Rights Law (Executive Law, Article 15)
* Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964
* Colorado Anti-Discrimination Act, C.R.S. §24-34-401 et seq., as amended by the Protecting Opportunities and Workers' Rights (POWR) Act
* All other applicable state and local anti-discrimination laws in jurisdictions where VisibleHand workforce members live or work

Where these laws differ, VisibleHand applies the standard that provides the greater protection to the workforce member.

## Scope and Policy Statement

1. Sexual harassment is prohibited. It is a form of employee misconduct, and sanctions are enforced against individuals who engage in sexual harassment and against supervisory and managerial personnel who knowingly allow such behavior to continue.
2. This policy applies to all workforce members, including employees, applicants for employment, officers, paid and unpaid interns, contractors, subcontractors, vendors, consultants, and any other person providing services to VisibleHand under a contract, regardless of immigration status. It protects workforce members from harassment by non-employees, and it prohibits harassment by VisibleHand workforce members directed at non-employees such as customer staff, residents, patients, and vendors.
3. This policy applies wherever VisibleHand work is performed, including VisibleHand offices and shared workspaces, remote and home work locations, customer facilities such as hospitals and senior care communities, conferences and trade shows, business travel, and company-sponsored social events.
4. This policy applies to conduct in any medium, including in person, by telephone, and through electronic communications such as email, Slack, text message, video conference, and social media, whether or not the conduct occurs on VisibleHand equipment or during working hours.
5. Every workforce member is expected to cooperate with an investigation conducted under this policy and to provide factual information to those conducting it.
6. Retaliation against any person who reports sexual harassment, assists in a report, or participates in an investigation or proceeding is unlawful and is prohibited by this policy.

## Definition of Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment is unwelcome conduct that is either of a sexual nature, or that is directed at an individual because of that individual's sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, transgender status, self-identified or perceived sex, pregnancy or a related condition, or because of the individual's non-conformance with sex stereotypes.

Conduct constitutes sexual harassment under this policy when:

1. It subjects an individual to inferior terms, conditions, or privileges of employment because of the individual's membership in one or more of the protected categories above. Conduct does not need to be severe or pervasive to violate this policy or New York and Colorado law. Conduct that a reasonable person with the same characteristics as the individual would consider a petty slight or trivial inconvenience is not covered.
2. It creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment, even where the conduct is not directed at the person who experiences it.
3. Submission to or rejection of the conduct is used, explicitly or implicitly, as a basis for an employment decision affecting the individual. This is often called quid pro quo harassment, and it can occur whether or not the person making the demand has formal authority over the individual.

Sexual harassment can occur between any individuals, regardless of their sex or gender, and regardless of their relative positions at VisibleHand.

## Examples of Prohibited Conduct

The following are examples of conduct that violates this policy. This list is illustrative and is not exhaustive.

* Physical acts of a sexual nature, including touching, pinching, patting, grabbing, brushing against, or poking another person's body; assault; and blocking or impeding movement.
* Unwanted sexual advances, propositions, or requests for dates or sexual favors, including repeated requests after the person has declined, and offering or implying a reward in exchange for a sexual favor.
* Verbal conduct such as sexual comments, jokes, innuendo, whistling, catcalling, remarks about a person's body, clothing, or appearance, questions or gossip about a person's sexual activity or history, and use of sexually degrading names.
* Comments or conduct directed at a person because of their gender identity or expression, including deliberately misgendering a person, deliberately using a former name, or making derogatory remarks about a person's transition, appearance, or restroom use.
* Sexual or discriminatory displays and publications, including images, cartoons, posters, screen savers, calendars, emoji, memes, GIFs, and messages sent or displayed through VisibleHand systems such as email, Slack, and video conferencing, or posted in any workspace.
* Hostile actions taken against a person because of their sex or a related protected category, including sabotaging work, interfering with assignments or advancement, excluding a person from meetings or communications, yelling, name-calling, bullying, and undermining a person's standing with customers.
* Retaliation against a person for reporting or opposing any of the conduct above, or for assisting in an investigation.

## Reporting a Complaint

1. Any workforce member who has been subjected to conduct that may violate this policy, or who witnesses or becomes aware of such conduct, is encouraged to report it promptly. Reports may be made verbally or in writing, using the complaint form included in this policy or any other written or verbal means.
2. Reports are made to the Policy Administrator. The Policy Administrator for this policy is the Chief Executive Officer, currently Matt Morris (matt@visiblehand.io, 720-500-5369). If the report concerns the Chief Executive Officer, or if the reporting person is for any reason not comfortable reporting to that person, the report is made instead to the Security Officer, currently Ian Schreuder, CTO (ian@visiblehand.io). VisibleHand designates an alternate recipient where both of these roles present a conflict.
3. A workforce member may also report anonymously, using the same anonymous reporting channel described in the Employees Policy. VisibleHand investigates anonymous reports, but an anonymous report may limit VisibleHand's ability to investigate fully or to inform the reporting person of the outcome.
4. Any supervisor, manager, or officer who receives a complaint, observes conduct that may violate this policy, or otherwise becomes aware of possible sexual harassment must report it to the Policy Administrator immediately, even if the person who experienced the conduct asks that no action be taken. Failing to report is itself a violation of this policy and is subject to discipline.
5. Customers, vendors, contractors, and other non-employees may report conduct by VisibleHand workforce members to the Policy Administrator at the contact information above.
6. Filing an internal complaint is not a prerequisite to seeking relief from an external agency or a court. The external remedies described below are available whether or not a workforce member uses this internal process, and the time limits for those remedies run independently of any internal investigation.

## Investigation Procedure

VisibleHand investigates every complaint promptly, thoroughly, and as confidentially as the investigation allows, and provides due process to all parties.

1. An investigation begins within five business days of the complaint being received and, where practicable, is completed within thirty days.
2. Where a complaint is made verbally, the person who receives it documents it in writing using the complaint form included in this policy and asks the complainant to review and sign the written record. An investigation proceeds whether or not the complainant chooses to sign.
3. The Policy Administrator conducts or designates the investigator. Where the Policy Administrator, the Security Officer, or another VisibleHand officer is a party to the complaint or has a conflict regarding it, VisibleHand retains an independent outside investigator such as outside counsel or a qualified human resources consultant.
4. The investigator interviews the complainant, the person accused, and any witnesses, and reviews relevant documents and records, including email, Slack messages, calendars, access logs, and personnel records.
5. Where necessary to protect the parties during the investigation, VisibleHand takes interim measures such as adjusting reporting relationships, schedules, or site assignments. Interim measures are not applied in a way that disadvantages the complainant.
6. The investigator prepares written documentation of the investigation that includes a list of the documents reviewed, a list of the individuals interviewed with a summary of each interview, a timeline of events, the basis for the conclusion reached, and the corrective action taken.
7. Both the complainant and the person accused are notified in writing of the outcome and are informed of their right to pursue the external remedies described below.
8. Investigation records are maintained by the Policy Administrator in a confidential file kept separately from routine personnel files. Records of complaints and investigations are retained for six years, consistent with the Data Retention Policy and with the record-keeping requirement of the Colorado Anti-Discrimination Act.
9. Information about a complaint and its investigation is disclosed only to those individuals who need it to investigate, to take corrective action, or to meet a legal obligation.

## Sanctions

1. A workforce member who is found to have engaged in sexual harassment is subject to corrective disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.
2. A supervisor, manager, or officer who knowingly allows sexual harassment to continue, who fails to report conduct as required by this policy, or who retaliates against a person for making a report, is subject to corrective disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.
3. Where the person who engaged in the conduct is a contractor, vendor, customer representative, or other non-employee, VisibleHand takes the corrective action available to it, which may include restricting site or system access, requiring replacement of the individual on VisibleHand work, notifying the contracting organization, and terminating the business relationship.
4. Conduct prohibited by this policy may also carry civil and criminal liability under federal, state, and local law.

## Prohibition on Retaliation

1. Retaliation against a person who engages in protected activity is unlawful under the New York State Human Rights Law, New York Labor Law §215, Title VII, the Colorado Anti-Discrimination Act, and other applicable laws, and is prohibited by this policy.
2. Protected activity includes making a complaint of sexual harassment internally or with any anti-discrimination agency, testifying or assisting in an investigation or proceeding, opposing conduct the person reasonably believes to be unlawful discrimination or harassment, reporting an incident to a supervisor, and encouraging a coworker to report.
3. Retaliation includes any action that would keep a reasonable person from making or supporting a complaint, such as termination, demotion, reduction in pay or hours, reassignment, exclusion from work opportunities, threats, and negative references.
4. A person who believes they have been retaliated against reports it in the same manner as any other complaint under this policy, and VisibleHand investigates it under the same procedure.

## Training

1. Every workforce member completes interactive sexual harassment prevention training within 30 days of beginning employment and annually thereafter. New supervisors and managers complete the supervisory portion of the training before or promptly upon assuming supervisory responsibility.
2. Training meets the New York State model training standards. It is interactive; explains sexual harassment consistent with guidance issued by the New York State Department of Labor and the Division of Human Rights; includes examples of prohibited conduct; describes the federal, state, and local statutory provisions concerning sexual harassment and the remedies available to victims; explains workforce members' rights of redress and the forums available to adjudicate complaints; and addresses the additional responsibilities of supervisors and managers and the conduct expected of them in responding to complaints.
3. A copy of this policy is provided in writing to each workforce member at hire, at each annual training, and whenever the policy is updated, and is published in the VisibleHand Employee Handbook and in this policy repository.
4. Training is provided in English and, where a workforce member's primary language is not English and a model translation is available, in that language.
5. Records of training completion are retained for every workforce member alongside the training records described in the Employees Policy.

## Legal Protections and External Remedies

Sexual harassment is unlawful and may also be a crime. In addition to the internal process described above, workforce members may seek relief from the following. VisibleHand does not require a workforce member to use the internal process first, and no VisibleHand policy or agreement limits a person's right to pursue these remedies.

* **New York State Division of Human Rights** - Enforces the New York State Human Rights Law. A complaint may be filed with the Division within three years of the alleged harassment, or an action may be brought directly in New York State court within three years. Contact (888) 392-3644 or dhr.ny.gov. Filing is free and an attorney is not required.
* **U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission** - Enforces Title VII. A charge must generally be filed within 300 days of the alleged harassment. Contact (800) 669-4000 or eeoc.gov. There is no fee to file.
* **Colorado Civil Rights Division** - Enforces the Colorado Anti-Discrimination Act. Contact (303) 894-2997 or the Division at the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies.
* **Local human rights agencies** - Many cities and counties have their own agencies and filing deadlines, including the New York City Commission on Human Rights at (212) 416-0197 for conduct occurring in New York City.
* **Local law enforcement** - Conduct involving unwanted physical contact, coerced physical confinement, or coerced sexual acts may constitute a crime. Contact local law enforcement or call 911.
